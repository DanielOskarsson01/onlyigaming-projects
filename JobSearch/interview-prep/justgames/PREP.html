7Questions to Ask Nathan — The Four Things That Decide Everything

Your job in this call is to find out what JustGames actually is so you can decide if the CMO role is real. Four areas decide it. The four are sequenced — the answer to each one shapes the next.

How to use this section

You don't need to ask all of these — pick the live ones in order, until you have enough signal. If Nathan can answer Areas A and B with specifics, this is a real opportunity. If he can answer A but not B, the founders haven't built the GTM yet (that's why they're hiring a CMO — but you need to know). If he can't answer A clearly, walk away politely.

AWhat is the product, and who is the customer? (B2B, B2C, or both?)

This question breaks everything else open. A "betting protocol" can be sold to three completely different customers, and the CMO job is unrecognizably different for each:

  • B2B to operators — JustGames is infrastructure (like Azuro). Customers are sportsbooks who plug in for liquidity / lower vig / odds feed. CMO sells to procurement/CTO/CEO at iGaming operators. Channel: conferences (ICE, SBC, iGB Live), industry press, cold outbound, partnerships.
  • B2C protocol with own frontend — JustGames runs its own consumer sportsbook on the protocol. CMO does what you did at MrGreen/ComeOn — brand, acquisition, CRM, retention, gamification, ambassadors-as-trial-drivers. Channel: paid where allowed, affiliates, ambassador-driven, content/community.
  • Protocol + third-party frontends — Azuro/Hyperliquid model. JustGames builds rails; independent teams build consumer brands. CMO has a hybrid job: market to frontend builders (DevRel, hackathons, grants) AND build awareness in the end-user community to give frontends customers to acquire.

The recruiter email is ambiguous. "Top ambassadors" and "$1M marketing budget" sound B2C. "Open-source protocol" sounds B2B/infrastructure. "Lowest fees" could be either. You need to know.

  1. "Walk me through the product. Is JustGames a B2C sportsbook running on its own protocol, a B2B infrastructure layer that licensed operators plug into, or both — protocol plus a flagship frontend?"

    The single most important question. The answer reshapes everything else.

  2. "Who's the customer the CMO is responsible for acquiring? End-users placing bets, operators integrating the protocol, or developer teams building frontends? Or is it all three at different stages?"

    Forces him to name the GTM target. If he says "all three," that's a $3.5M-doesn't-cover-it flag.

  3. "Is there a thought about JustGames running its own branded sportsbook on top of the protocol — a flagship B2C product? Or is the strategy strictly to enable other operators?"

    Pins down whether you'd be building a brand or selling to procurement.

BWho exactly is the customer, and can they be served legally?

Once you know what the product is, ask who can buy it and where they're allowed to use it. "Lowest fees" is irrelevant if the customer can't legally touch the product.

  • B2C: which countries can players legally access from? MGA, UKGC, German GlüStV, Italian ADM, Brazilian Bets? Or offshore-only, geo-blocking everywhere licensed and serving the gray markets (LATAM ex-Brazil, SEA, Africa)?
  • B2B: selling to licensed operators (MGA, UKGC, ADM, Brazilian Bets, Ontario AGCO) who'll want certifications (GLI-19, ISO 27001, RNG audits, MGA approval letters)? Or gray-market crypto operators (Curaçao sub-license era, offshore crypto books) who don't need those certs but have shrinking access as Curaçao reforms close the loophole?
  • Protocol: is the protocol itself licensable, or by design unlicensable (decentralized, no central operator)?

  1. "Geographic strategy — which countries does the product target on day one, and which does it geo-block? Are you operating in licensed regulated markets, the gray markets, or both with different stacks?"

    The honest answer reveals the entire regulatory posture. "Global, no geo-blocks" = gray-market, full stop. Know that's the bet.

  2. "On the B2B side, are you targeting licensed operators (MGA, UKGC, ADM, Brazilian Bets, Ontario) or gray-market crypto operators? Because the sales motion and the product certifications are different worlds."

    Signals you understand the regulated B2B landscape and forces him to commit to a tier.

  3. "Can this product be licensed at all? Is the protocol itself something you'd put through MGA Class 2 or Brazilian Bets, or is the model deliberately outside licensing — operate as a protocol, let frontends carry licenses where they need them?"

    The architecture question. Some protocols are designed to be unlicensable; others wrap a licensed entity around them. Affects everything from marketing language to whether you can put a logo on a Premier League sleeve.

  4. "What does the legal team say about Brazil specifically? Bets framework launched 2024 and the regulated market is massive — are you going through Bets licensing or geo-blocking Brazil at launch?"

    Brazil is the marquee LATAM opportunity. Answer reveals whether they have real legal counsel or are vibes-on-LATAM.

CThe ambassadors. Who are they and what are they actually for?

The recruiter said "top ambassadors, already signed." Given Igor's network, they're almost certainly poker pros or crypto influencers (Phil Hellmuth, Daniel Negreanu, Doug Polk, Phil Galfond on poker; Vitalik, crypto KOLs on Web3). Possibly Liv Boeree. Maybe pro-EA figures.

  • B2C ambassadors → trial drivers. Their audience comes and tries the product. CMO measures CAC, conversion, retention. Standard playbook from your MrGreen days.
  • B2B ambassadors → almost meaningless. Operators don't care that Phil Hellmuth shills a protocol. They care about latency, liquidity, settlement, certifications. Useful for conference panels and dinners — not for closing deals.
  • Crypto/protocol ambassadors → distribution and credibility in the onchain community. They drive token holders, frontend builders, governance participation. Different from sportsbook ambassadors entirely.

  1. "Who are the ambassadors? Poker world, crypto/Web3, sports, or a mix?"

    Concrete. Forces him to name names or admit he doesn't know.

  2. "What is the ambassador's actual role on launch — drive end-user signups, lend credibility to operator sales, or distribute through their content/social audience? Because the work I'd do with a poker pro to drive B2C trial is completely different from how I'd use them at a B2B conference."

    This is your sharpened question. Surfaces whether the team has thought through ambassador economics or just signed people because Igor knows them.

  3. "If this is B2B, what would an ambassador actually do for the company? Operators don't buy infrastructure from Phil Hellmuth — they buy from a sales team with certifications and references. Is the ambassador role for awareness, or do you see it as a sales channel?"

    Polite version of "Igor may have signed ambassadors without a clear job for them in B2B." Surfaces the gap if it exists.

  4. "Are the ambassadors equity holders, paid spokespeople, or token allocation? The deal structure shapes how aggressively I can ask them to work."

    Real CMO question. Tells you how much leverage you'd actually have over them.

DThe $1M marketing budget. What does Igor want it spent on?

The $1M number is the most concrete thing in the email — and the most testable. Roughly four buckets a $1M launch budget could go into. The founder's instinct here reveals whether they understand the marketing reality:

  • B2B operator acquisition — conferences (SBC Barcelona, ICE Barcelona, iGB Live Amsterdam, SBC Lisbon, SiGMA — booth + sponsorship €50-200K per major event), trade press, cold outbound BDR team ($200-400K/yr per BDR), industry awards, key account partnerships.
  • B2C player acquisition — paid digital where legal (Google/Meta closed for unlicensed; programmatic limited), affiliate networks (Income Access, Better Collective, Catena Media — 30-50% rev share or CPA $50-300/depositor), influencer/KOL deals, content/SEO, referral programs.
  • Crypto-native distribution — Crypto Twitter/X, Telegram/Discord, points/airdrop campaigns, KOL deals with crypto influencers, hackathon sponsorships if developer-targeting.
  • Brand and event marketing — sponsorships (sports teams/leagues — closed without license), launch events, PR campaigns, content production.

A serious founder will have at least a rough split in mind. A vibes founder will say "growth marketing" or "spend it where it works."

  1. "What does Igor see the $1M being spent on, roughly? Conferences and industry sales? Influencer deals? Crypto KOLs and points campaigns? Paid acquisition? Cold outbound to operators? I want to understand the channel mix he's already imagining."

    The single most diagnostic question of the call. The answer tells you what Igor thinks the business is.

  2. "For example — what's the rough split between B2B sales motion (conferences, trade press, cold outbound to operators) versus B2C acquisition (paid, affiliates, ambassador-driven trial)? Because $1M is plenty for one but tight for both."

    Forces a B2B/B2C ratio. The number tells you everything.

  3. "Is the $1M for pre-launch awareness only, or does it cover the first 6 months of operating spend? Because '$1M launch budget' and '$1M for the year' are very different planning exercises."

    Tests whether the founders have thought about pacing. First-time founders often mistake one-off launch spend for ongoing marketing budget.

  4. "What channels are explicitly off the table given the regulatory posture? Google and Meta are closed for unlicensed gambling brands; sports sponsorships need an operator license. Has the team mapped that, or am I going to need to make those tradeoffs?"

    Signals you know what's actually closed to you and surfaces whether they've thought it through.

Close-out (after the four areas — 90 seconds max)

  1. "What's the legal entity name and jurisdiction of incorporation?"

    Basic due diligence. Establishes seriousness.

  2. "How is the $3.5M structured, who's the lead investor, and what's the milestone to Series A?"

    Funding reality check.

  3. "What's the process from here, timeline, and who I'd meet at each stage?"

  4. "What would make a candidate stand out to Igor in particular?"

    Lets Nathan share intel for the next stage if you advance.

Comp range goes in section 8 — don't dance, but don't make it the loudest note of the screen either. Ask once, clearly, then move on.

8Compensation / Deal-Shape Notes

You're a senior exec talking to a pre-seed/seed crypto-betting protocol. The comp structure is fundamentally different from a regulated EU operator. Walk in knowing this.

What to expect (realistic ranges, 2026 market)

  • Base salary: $180-250K USD for a stealth pre-Series A CMO. Could be lower for crypto-native shop ($150K) or higher for an experienced operator like you ($250-300K).
  • Equity: 1-3% of common, 4-year vest with 1-year cliff, typical pre-seed CMO. Closer to 2-3% if you're the first commercial hire and not just one of the early leadership.
  • Token allocation (if token exists): this is the wild card. Crypto founders sometimes offer 0.5-2% of token supply with multi-year vest and cliff. Could be worth 0, could be worth a lot. Treat tokens as lottery tickets, not comp.
  • Cash bonus: probably none at this stage. Performance bonus tied to launch milestones is reasonable to negotiate.
  • Severance / acceleration: critical at this stage. Ask for double-trigger acceleration on change of control and at least 6-month severance if terminated without cause.

What to ask for at the screen

Don't negotiate at the screen. Just gather. But signal range:

Signal range

"For context — for a role like this I'd expect base in the $200-280K range plus meaningful equity (2-3% common) plus any token allocation if one exists. Severance and acceleration would matter at this stage given the runway. Does that sit within the range Igor's authorized?"

  • If Nathan says "yes that's in range" → good signal, advance.
  • If Nathan says "we're thinking more like $150K base, 0.5% equity, no token, no severance" → that's a flag the founders are either underfunded or undervaluing the role. Probe.
  • If Nathan deflects ("Igor would discuss directly") → fair, but don't let him skip the question. "Understood. Can you tell me even a range so we don't waste each other's time?"

Risk factors to weigh into comp

  • Regulatory wipeout risk: ACMA blocks, MGA refuses, Brazil shuts the door → company can be revenue-zero overnight. Demand more equity to offset.
  • Token dilution risk: if token launches, your common equity may dilute. Want anti-dilution or token allocation in addition to equity.
  • Founder team risk: poker-world founders may not bring iGaming operating maturity. You absorb the operational deficit. Demand title/scope clarity.
  • Runway risk: $3.5M is short. If Series A doesn't close in 9-12 months, you're job-hunting again. Bake severance into the offer.

9STAR Stories — Three Tailored to Likely Questions

For Nathan: "The pattern at JustGames will be different because of the protocol model and the crypto-native channels, but the org-building methodology — build BI first, prove value with MVPs, separate brand from performance once you have signal — translates directly. And I've done it on small founding teams."

Story 2 — 0-to-1 brand launch on a tight budget with ambassadors (MrGreen)

Likely question: "Tell me about launching a brand from nothing, especially with personalities or ambassadors involved."

Situation 2009. MrGreen was seven people in a two-bedroom apartment in Malta. No brand, no CRM, no customers, no playbook. We had a character — "Mr Green" — and conviction that personality-led iGaming branding would beat generic operator branding.

Task Build the brand from zero. Get to product-market fit in Sweden first, then scale.

Action I wrote the brand book, the CI, the communication manual, the tone of voice. Within a year of that CRM overhaul, we'd grown active users by nearly 400% — by replacing batch-and-blast with behavior-triggered, segmented communication.

Result MrGreen went from 7 to 250+ people and NASDAQ-listed. Best casino site in Sweden five years running (Internetworld). Online Casino of the Year 2011 and 2013. The brand was successful enough that 888 acquired it in 2018.

For Nathan: "The relevant lesson for JustGames is: when you have ambassadors and a thin budget, the brand is the multiplier. The ambassadors get you trial; the brand, CRM, and gamification get you retention. I'd want to know what role Igor wants the ambassadors to play, because that decides what kind of brand we build around them."

Story 3 — Crypto-native operator from zero (Coinhero)

Likely question: "Tell me about Coinhero — what's relevant for a crypto-paying betting product?"

Situation 2023. I was brought in by the Coinhero founders as CEO/founder to build a crypto-native iGaming operator from scratch — casino, sportsbook, and cryptocurrency rails. No team, no licenses, no brand, no platform.

Task Full operational ownership from zero. Strategy, licensing across multiple jurisdictions, recruitment, brand, product, vendor selection, and team management. Build the company so it could be either a single-brand operator or the foundation for multi-brand expansion.
