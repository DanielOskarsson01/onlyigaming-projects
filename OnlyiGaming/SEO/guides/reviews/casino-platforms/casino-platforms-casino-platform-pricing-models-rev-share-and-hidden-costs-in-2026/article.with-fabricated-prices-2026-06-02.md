## Pricing Models Explained

The casino platform market runs on four main commercial models. Each one shifts risk and cost timing differently.

### Revenue Share

Revenue share means the platform takes a percentage of your gross gaming revenue (GGR) monthly. Typical ranges are 10-25%, with most deals landing between 12-18%.

**Pros:**
- Lower upfront capital requirement
- Platform success aligned with operator success
- Easier to budget as a percentage of revenue
- Less financial risk during launch phase

**Cons:**
- Becomes expensive at scale (millions in annual fees for successful brands)
- Platform controls the billing metric (GGR calculation)
- Harder to predict long-term unit economics
- Creates incentive misalignment around operator margins

**Best for:** New operators, crypto brands, or anyone launching in unproven markets where upfront capital preservation matters more than long-term cost optimization.

### Fixed License Fee

Monthly or annual licensing fees regardless of revenue performance. Enterprise deals often combine multiple modules into bundled pricing.

**Pros:**
- Predictable cost structure for financial planning
- No revenue sharing means better unit economics at scale
- More negotiation leverage on renewal terms
- Cleaner margin calculations for investors

**Cons:**
- High upfront and ongoing costs regardless of performance
- Platform has less incentive to support your growth
- Cash flow pressure during low-revenue periods
- Typically requires minimum commitment terms

**Best for:** Established operators with predictable revenue streams, multi-brand portfolios, or businesses targeting high-volume markets where revenue share costs become prohibitive.

### Hybrid Models

Combination of lower revenue share (5-10%) plus monthly platform fees. Some deals include performance tiers that shift the ratio as you scale.

**Pros:**
- Balances upfront cost management with long-term economics
- Platform maintains growth incentive without maximum cost exposure
- Can include volume discounts or performance bonuses
- Flexibility to renegotiate terms based on actual performance

**Cons:**
- More complex contract terms and billing
- Requires accurate revenue forecasting for budget planning
- Platform may push you toward the higher-margin pricing tier
- Exit costs can be higher due to multiple fee components

**Best for:** Mid-market operators with established revenue but growth plans, or businesses entering multiple markets with different performance expectations.

### Transaction-Based Pricing

Payment per game round, bet, or player action. Less common for full platforms but used for specific modules like payment orchestration or fraud prevention.

**Pros:**
- Direct cost correlation with platform usage
- Easy to calculate ROI per transaction
- No revenue sharing or minimum commitments
- Scales perfectly with business volume

**Cons:**
- Can become expensive for high-frequency games
- Billing complexity for multiple transaction types
- Less predictable monthly costs
- May incentivize platform to increase transaction volume regardless of profitability

**Best for:** Operators with specific module needs rather than full platform requirements, or businesses with highly variable transaction patterns.

Real casino platform costs follow this formula: **License Fee + Implementation + Hidden Costs + Opportunity Cost (time to market delay)**. The hidden costs typically exceed the platform license by 100-200% in year one.

**Implementation timeline impact:** Every month of delayed launch costs you both ongoing operational overhead and missed revenue opportunity. **Exit and migration costs:** Switching platforms typically costs 60-80% of a new implementation. Your player database, transaction history, and compliance documentation are locked in the current system. Budget 6-12 months and $200K-$500K to migrate a live operation to a new platform.

**Scale economics:** Revenue share models that seem affordable at $1M annual GGR become brutal at $20M+ GGR. A 15% revenue share on $20M GGR costs $3M annually - enough to license multiple fixed-fee enterprise platforms. Most operators renegotiate or migrate when they hit these scaling thresholds.

The platforms know this math. That's why revenue share deals often include volume discounts that kick in precisely when the operator starts considering alternatives.

## Red Flags in Pricing Conversations

Watch for these warning signs during vendor pricing discussions:

- **"Custom pricing only" with no ballpark range:** Legitimate vendors can give you rough ranges based on your described requirements. Refusing to provide any guidance usually means they're planning to price based on your perceived budget rather than actual cost.

- **Setup fees that exceed 50% of annual licensing cost:** High setup fees often hide poor implementation processes or understaffed onboarding teams. If it costs $200K to set up a $300K annual license, question whether their platform is actually implementation-ready.

- **Revenue share calculations that exclude certain game types:** Some deals exclude live dealer, progressive jackpots, or specific providers from revenue share calculations, then charge separate fees for those games. Read the fine print on what counts toward your revenue share base.

- **Bundled pricing that obscures per-module costs:** All-in-one pricing makes it impossible to evaluate cost per capability or negotiate specific component changes. This pricing strategy often hides weak modules behind strong ones.

- **Minimum GGR commitments buried in contract terms:** Some deals require minimum monthly revenue commitments regardless of actual performance. Miss the minimum, and you pay the difference as a penalty fee.

- **Payment processing markups disguised as "platform fees":** Platform providers sometimes add 1-2% markup on payment processing costs without clearly disclosing this as a profit center separate from the core platform license.

The best pricing conversations include detailed cost breakdowns, clear examples of additional fees, and specific timelines with milestone-based payment schedules.

## Common Questions

### Why won't casino platform vendors publish standard pricing?

Platform deals are highly customized based on target markets, expected volume, compliance requirements, and integration complexity. A crypto-only brand launching in Curacao has completely different needs than a multi-market operator seeking UK and German licenses. Published pricing would either be meaningless (too generic) or limiting (can't account for deal complexity).

The real reason is competitive positioning. Vendors use custom pricing to maximize deal value based on your budget, timeline pressure, and available alternatives. Publishing prices would enable easier comparison shopping and reduce pricing flexibility.

### How do I compare revenue share versus fixed fee at different scale levels?

Calculate the breakeven point where revenue share costs equal fixed licensing fees. If a platform charges 15% revenue share or $400K fixed annual license, you break even at $2.67M annual GGR. Below that threshold, revenue share is cheaper. Above it, fixed licensing wins.

Factor in growth timeline: if you expect to cross the breakeven threshold within 18 months, negotiate fixed pricing from the start. Migration costs make it expensive to switch pricing models later. Also consider that revenue share deals often include volume discounts that change the math at higher scales.

### What's typically included versus extra in platform licensing deals?

Core platform software is usually included: player account management, game integration framework, basic bonus tools, standard reporting, and API access. Almost everything else costs extra: payment processing, game content, compliance tools, premium support, custom development, hosting infrastructure, and third-party integrations.

Think of platform licensing like buying a car chassis - you get the frame, but you're paying separately for the engine (games), transmission (payments), navigation (compliance), and insurance (support). The platform provides the foundation, not the complete operational stack.

### How do Brazilian market entry requirements affect platform costs?

Law 14.790/2023 requires platforms to maintain Brazilian player data within national borders, implement specific responsible gaming controls, and integrate with government monitoring systems. This adds $50K-$150K in compliance setup costs and ongoing infrastructure requirements.

Not all platforms support Brazilian compliance requirements out of the box. Budget additional integration work if your platform provider doesn't have existing SECAP (Sistema de Execução de Controle de Apostas e Prêmios) connectivity and data localization capabilities.

### When should I negotiate revenue share versus seeking fixed-fee pricing?

Choose revenue share when you need capital preservation during launch, enter unproven markets, or launch experimental brands where failure is possible. The downside protection matters more than long-term cost optimization.

Negotiate fixed fees when you have predictable revenue streams, plan multiple brand launches on the same platform, or target high-volume markets where revenue share costs become prohibitive. Fixed pricing also gives you cleaner unit economics for investor reporting.

### What payment and crypto handling adds to total platform costs?

Payment processing typically adds 3-8% of total transaction volume regardless of your platform choice. Crypto adds complexity: wallet management, exchange rate handling, regulatory compliance across multiple jurisdictions, and specialized KYC requirements.

Budget an additional $30K-$100K for crypto-capable payment orchestration if your platform doesn't include native cryptocurrency support. The ongoing operational overhead of managing crypto liquidity and compliance typically adds 15-20% to your payment processing costs versus fiat-only operations.
