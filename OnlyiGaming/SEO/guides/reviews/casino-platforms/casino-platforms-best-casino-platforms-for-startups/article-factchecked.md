## Contract Minimums and Flexibility

Startup cash flow is unpredictable. Platforms designed for established operators often require 36-month commitments and annual payments that assume steady revenue. Startup-friendly providers offer more flexible contract terms that align with early-stage business realities.

**12-month minimums** are common for startup-focused providers like NuxGame and GR8 Tech. This provides enough time for market validation without creating excessive commitment for unproven business models. Most operators know within 12 months whether their market approach is working.

**24-month contracts** are sometimes associated with providers like BlueOcean Gaming and SOFTSWISS, reflecting their operational investment in client onboarding and support. This longer commitment often comes with better commercial terms or more comprehensive service levels.

**Enterprise contracts** from EveryMatrix may include revenue share models, with setup costs typically ranging from €75,000 to €200,000. These longer terms typically offer better pricing but create significant commitment for uncertain startup operations.

**Payment timing flexibility** varies between providers. Some require annual payments upfront. Others offer quarterly payments with small premiums. A few provide monthly billing that aligns with startup cash flow preferences, though usually at higher effective rates.

**The growth clause trap:** Some contracts include automatic rate increases based on volume thresholds or operational complexity. A contract that seems affordable at $50K monthly GGR might become expensive at $300K monthly GGR if it includes automatic tier upgrades.

**Performance guarantees** flow both directions. Providers expect minimum activity levels or monthly payments. Operators should expect uptime guarantees, support response SLAs, and feature availability commitments. Balanced contracts protect both parties.

**Early termination for cause** should include provider performance failures, not just operator payment defaults. Contracts that allow termination only for non-payment create power imbalances that favor providers over startup operators.

**What to negotiate:** Monthly rather than annual payments, performance-based termination clauses, clear volume threshold definitions, and written confirmation of contract terms at renewal. Startup contracts should protect operators during uncertain growth phases.

**Red flags in contract terms:** Automatic renewals without notification periods, termination clauses that heavily favor providers, or volume commitments that assume linear growth patterns.
