## Migration Path and Exit Economics

Startup platforms should enable growth, not trap it. The most expensive platform mistakes happen when successful operators outgrow their initial choice but face prohibitive migration costs or technical lock-in. Understanding exit economics upfront prevents costly surprises as you scale.

**Revenue-sharing models** create natural pressure to migrate as you grow. NuxGame, BlueOcean Gaming, and GR8 Tech all expect successful clients to eventually outgrow their offerings. The question is whether they facilitate clean migrations or make leaving expensive through technical dependencies.

**NuxGame provides standard data exports** but doesn't offer migration services to help you move to other platforms. Your player database, transaction history, and operational data are portable, but you'll need technical resources to implement them on a new platform. This is manageable for operators with development capabilities.

**SOFTSWISS offers migration paths** within their ecosystem - from revenue-sharing to license fee models, or from basic to advanced platform configurations. This reduces migration friction but keeps you within their technology stack. For many startups, this internal scaling path eliminates the need for external migrations.

**The data portability question** varies significantly between providers. Some platforms export clean, standardized data formats. Others provide raw database dumps that require significant technical work to implement elsewhere. Test data export capabilities during your evaluation period, not after you've decided to migrate.

**Contract termination costs** often include remaining minimum payments, data export fees, and technical wind-down charges. A 24-month contract with 18 months remaining might cost 50-100% of remaining payments to exit early. Factor these costs into your growth planning.

**Technical lock-in** happens gradually through custom integrations, proprietary APIs, and operational dependencies. Platforms that encourage heavy customization often make migration more difficult. Simple, standardized implementations migrate more easily but offer less competitive differentiation.

**Migration timeline reality:** Moving from one platform to another typically takes 12-16 weeks for production-ready deployment. This includes data migration, payment provider re-certification, regulatory notifications, and parallel testing. Budget for 3-4 months of double-platform costs during transitions.

**What to get in writing:** Specific data export formats and timelines, termination costs as dollar amounts rather than percentages, technical requirements for migration assistance, and timeline commitments for data availability after contract termination.

## Contract Minimums and Flexibility

Startup cash flow is unpredictable. Platforms designed for established operators often require 36-month commitments and annual payments that assume steady revenue. Startup-friendly providers offer more flexible contract terms that align with early-stage business realities.

**12-month minimums** are standard for true startup-focused providers like NuxGame and GR8 Tech. This provides enough time for market validation without creating excessive commitment for unproven business models. Most operators know within 12 months whether their market approach is working.

**24-month contracts** from providers like BlueOcean Gaming and SOFTSWISS reflect higher operational investment in client onboarding and support. The longer commitment often comes with better commercial terms or more comprehensive service levels.

**36-month enterprise contracts** from EveryMatrix and established providers assume mature operations with predictable cash flow. These longer terms typically offer better pricing but create significant commitment for uncertain startup operations.

**Payment timing flexibility** varies between providers. Some require annual payments upfront. Others offer quarterly payments with small premiums. A few provide monthly billing that aligns with startup cash flow preferences, though usually at higher effective rates.

**The growth clause trap:** Some contracts include automatic rate increases based on volume thresholds or operational complexity. A contract that seems affordable at $50K monthly GGR might become expensive at $300K monthly GGR if it includes automatic tier upgrades.

**Performance guarantees** flow both directions. Providers expect minimum activity levels or monthly payments. Operators should expect uptime guarantees, support response SLAs, and feature availability commitments. Balanced contracts protect both parties.

**Early termination for cause** should include provider performance failures, not just operator payment defaults. Contracts that allow termination only for non-payment create power imbalances that favor providers over startup operators.

**What to negotiate:** Monthly rather than annual payments, performance-based termination clauses, clear volume threshold definitions, and written confirmation of contract terms at renewal. Startup contracts should protect operators during uncertain growth phases.

**Red flags in contract terms:** Automatic renewals without notification periods, termination clauses that heavily favor providers, or volume commitments that assume linear growth patterns.
