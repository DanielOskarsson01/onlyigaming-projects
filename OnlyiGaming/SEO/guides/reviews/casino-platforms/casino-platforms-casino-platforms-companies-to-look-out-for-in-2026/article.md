## How to Evaluate a Newcomer

Working with emerging platform providers requires different due diligence than evaluating established vendors. These companies may lack the extensive track records and public references that make established platforms easy to verify. However, there are specific steps that reveal whether a newcomer is ready for production deployment.

1.  **Request live operator references** - not just client lists, but operators willing to speak directly about their experience. Any legitimate platform provider should connect you with 2-3 current clients running live money operations. If they can't or won't, that's disqualifying regardless of how impressive their technology demonstration appears.

2.  **Verify regulatory status independently** - don't rely on vendor claims about licensing and compliance. Check directly with gaming authorities to confirm license status, and review any public enforcement actions or compliance issues. Emerging providers often overstate their regulatory readiness.

3.  **Test the sandbox environment extensively** - demand access to fully functional test systems that mirror production capabilities. Many newcomers have impressive demos that don't reflect real-world performance. Test edge cases, high-volume scenarios, and integration complexity before committing.

4.  **Examine contract exit terms carefully** - newer providers often include restrictive clauses that make migration difficult if the relationship doesn't work out. Pay particular attention to data portability, player database ownership, and termination notice periods. You want the option to leave without losing your entire business.

5.  **Validate financial stability** - request recent financial statements or evidence of adequate funding to support long-term operations. Platform relationships typically span multiple years, and vendor financial problems become operator problems quickly. Look for confirmed funding rounds, profitable operations, or parent company backing.

6.  **Pilot before full deployment** - even if a vendor passes all other checks, start with limited scope to prove the relationship works operationally. Run a single-market pilot or limited game selection for 3-6 months before expanding to full production deployment.

Start with a pilot program rather than betting your entire launch on an unproven platform relationship. The potential advantages of working with emerging providers - better pricing, faster feature development, more personalized support - are real, but only if you verify their capabilities thoroughly before committing.

## Red FLAGS When Working With Newcomers

Several warning signs indicate a company isn't ready for production deployment, regardless of how compelling their pitch or competitive their pricing. These red flags should end evaluation immediately rather than being treated as concerns to address later.

**No verifiable client references willing to speak publicly** - Legitimate platform providers have current clients who will discuss their experience with prospective operators. If a vendor can't connect you with reference clients, or if their "clients" are all in pilot phases or unwilling to be contacted, the platform isn't ready for production use.

**Sandbox environment that doesn't match promised capabilities** - Many emerging providers have impressive sales presentations that don't reflect actual product functionality. The test environment should demonstrate every feature promised in production, not "coming soon" or "available in next release" versions.

**Pricing significantly below market rates** - Platform services have real costs for compliance, infrastructure, and support. Pricing that seems too good to be true often indicates the vendor is subsidizing operations to acquire clients and and may not be sustainable long-term. This creates risk that pricing will increase dramatically or the company will struggle financially.

**Founders without relevant industry experience** - Platform development requires deep understanding of gaming regulations, operator needs, and technical requirements. Founders who come from completely unrelated industries without hiring experienced iGaming leadership often underestimate the complexity of building production-ready solutions.

**Unwillingness to provide detailed technical documentation** - Professional platform providers have comprehensive API documentation, integration guides, and technical specifications they share during evaluation. Vendors who are secretive about technical details or promise documentation "after contract signing" typically have incomplete or poorly designed systems.

These aren't minor concerns to negotiate around - they're fundamental indicators that a vendor isn't ready for production deployment. The excitement of working with innovative newcomers shouldn't override basic due diligence requirements.
